# Gameplay guide

"Stronghold Protocol: Alliance" is auto-chess + tower defense: during the rest phase you recruit operators, arrange your formation and fit gear at the Dispatch Center; during combat your operators deploy automatically and fend off the enemies pouring in from the red door, and enemies that leak through cost you Life Points (LP). In Alliance Simulation 1–4 players **co-op** (no PvP), and you win by holding out to round 14 and taking down the enemy leader.

The numbers in this guide come from the official data tables (`data/config.json` and so on) and the [research](research/00-INDEX.md); the in-game 📖 "How to play" at the bottom left has the official tutorial images (needs local extraction). For controls, see [README · Controls](../README.md#controls).

## 1. The flow of a match

```
Confirm match info (alliance 25 s) → Strategy draft (turn-based) → Protocol start
→ Each round: [Draft] → Rest phase → Combat → [Unite] → Settlement
→ Round 14: Final Assault →(if conditions met) Round 15: Hidden Core → Settlement title
```

| Phase | Description |
|---|---|
| Confirm match info | This match's map (Standard Simulation is fixed to "Battlefield #01"; Hazard picks at random from 8 maps, Peril / Ultimate from the 7 that exclude Battlefield #01), the enemy faction, the **disabled alliances and their operators**, and a leader preview. Everyone continues once they confirm; Alliance Simulation allows up to 25 seconds; when you are the only player (Solo Simulation, or an Alliance Simulation with only AI teammates) there is no time limit. |
| Strategy draft | The 40 strategies (squad leaders) decide the **starting Life Points (20–45)** and one permanent rule. Alliance: players pick in turn in a random order, **30 seconds each**, with the same countdown shown at the top right and in "Deciding"; AI teammates pick instantly; you can't repeat a teammate's pick, and each player may **skip once** (moved to the end); on timeout you auto-pick your **currently selected** strategy (or Warfarin if a teammate already took it). Solo: free choice, no time limit; an Alliance Simulation with only you as a player is also untimed. While picking you can click "View disabled alliances and operators" below the decision order on the left to review the alliances from "Confirm match info" (grayed out, with the disabled count) and this match's disabled operators again: view only, the countdown keeps running, and the bottom also shows whose turn it is and the seconds left; close it with "Close", by clicking outside the window or by pressing Esc, and it closes automatically when the next player's turn comes. |
| Draft | Appears before some rounds begin: in an alliance, 6 cards total are picked one each in turn (30 seconds for the first player, 16 for the rest; untimed when you are the only player), solo is 1-of-3. Cards include gear, funds, operators, alliance layers, bounties and so on; a **bounty card** adds bounty enemies to your combat, with blue text saying how long it lasts: "next battle" / "the next two battles" appear for only one or two battles and then stop; the official "multi-round bounty" (whose original text reads "every battle thereafter") is also treated as "the next two battles" here, appearing for only two battles (such as the whip's Arts Master A2 · Multi-round Tactical Training). Each round's bounty cards follow the official match screenshots: round 3 is 6 "next two battles" bounties (3 worth 1 fund, 2 worth 2, 1 worth 3); round 9 is each leader's "next battle" bounty and "Originium Slug · Special Training"; round 11 of Peril / Ultimate is usually bounty cards (it can also be the Secret Shop or Tactical Decision), where the bounties usually include 1 elite giant beast and the rest are each faction's "next battle" bounties. Whoever downs a bounty enemy (teammates in the Unite Phase count too) gets funds, and the effects panel on the right says "N battles left". The 6 pieces of gear in the **Secret Shop** are all free and, as officially, two identical pieces can appear (each counts as its own card and can be picked separately); the round-11 Secret Shop (Peril / Ultimate) follows the official layout: 2 tier-VI, at least 1 tier-V and 1 Alliance Coin, while the earlier-round Secret Shops of Standard / Hazard can show gear of any tier. **Tactical Decision**, as officially, can also show two identical cards (two "Supply" cards appeared in an official screenshot; each counts as its own card and can be picked separately, each takes effect once, and two players each picking a team-wide card stacks the effect); the round-11 Tactical Decision (Peril / Ultimate), per official screenshots, only offers buff cards such as Oath, Reinforcement, Outfitting / Wealth / Supply / Refit / Sublimation, not enemy-weakening or battlefield-evolution cards. Each card has an icon and name at the top left and its full effect below (the same official description as the detail card, shown on phones too); a card already taken is dimmed and shows the portrait of the player who took it at the top right (hover for the name). Picking a card takes **two clicks**: the first only selects it (gold frame + "Confirm selection · click again"), and only clicking the same card again or "Confirm selection" at the top right takes effect; click another card to switch, or press Esc / click a blank area to cancel. |
| Rest phase | Buy, arrange, fit gear, freeze / refresh, upgrade the Dispatch Center; alliances have a countdown (65–215 seconds, rising with the round); Solo Simulation, and an Alliance Simulation with only you as a player (with only AI teammates), are untimed. Combat starts early once everyone is "Ready", and it can be canceled before everyone is ready. |
| Combat | Each player fights on their own battlefield (combat is simulated in your browser at 2× speed). Operators deploy automatically in **top-to-bottom, left-to-right** order, consuming Deployment Points (starting at 10, +1/s). The time limit is 45–115 seconds (real seconds, i.e. the on-screen countdown), and enemies still alive when time runs out count as leaks. When an enemy enters the blue door, the top bar's target LP **immediately** shows the value after the deduction (red text + "−N", at most 10 per round), finalized at settlement; the Unite Phase is marked "Uniting", and the part a teammate holds off is not deducted in the end. The team panel on the left likewise shows a teammate's pending LP loss. A knocked-down operator **stays on the tile it fell on** (in a downed pose, slightly grayed), and redeploys on that same tile later (just like officially; one that fell on another operator's or summon's starting tile returns to its own starting tile and lies there, even if the summon is already gone); the tile a downed operator occupies **is not taken up**, and raid-alliance redeploys, reinforcements and summons all avoid it. The circle above its head is the redeploy countdown: a green ring + seconds; turning into a yellow "DP" means not enough Deployment Points. The row below the health bar is **elemental damage** (same as the original: an element icon + a white bar): operators are colored discs with an icon, enemies are small solid-color discs, and the white bar on the right is the remaining elemental value, decreasing right-to-left like a health bar, and the instant the white bar empties is the gauge break (when a large elemental hit removes a chunk at once the white bar also drops a chunk at once — e.g. a Skimming Sea Drifter's hit is about 25%–50% each); after the break it enters a burst cooldown (burn / neural / erosion 10 s, enemy erosion 8 s, apoptosis 15 s), during which the white bar turns the **element's color** (neural teal, erosion blue, burn orange-red, apoptosis gray-purple) and slowly refills while the icon flashes — a colored bar means the gauge has already broken and the burst effect is being applied, not the elemental value remaining; during this period no element accumulates and none can be healed, and it is cleared to zero afterwards. Elemental burst deals **sourceless damage** that ignores an operator's damage bonuses, penetration and gear bonuses (e.g. Underflow's burn burst against a Seaborn is a fixed 7000), and the kill is still credited to the operator who caused the break; elemental fragility only raises elemental damage, it does not speed up the elemental gauge; fragility does not affect elemental damage. An enemy already killed will not have further elemental damage applied. |
| Unite | See section 5. |
| Settlement | Deduct LP, pay out bounty funds, settle layers; a player at LP ≤ 0 is eliminated (their operators return to the shared pool). |

**Disconnecting and leaving**: in Alliance Simulation, reopening the page within 10 minutes of a disconnect returns you to your seat; while you are away your formation keeps fighting automatically and drafts / rest phases auto-pick / ready-up on time, but nothing is bought for you (to let an AI play for you, choose "Step out (AI takeover)" under "Leave simulation"; the AI takeover won't destroy your items to make room, and when the Reserve is full it sells benched operators to place items). A Solo Simulation's rest phase and draft are untimed, and after a disconnect or closing the page you can continue **within 24 hours** by opening it in the same browser; a Solo Simulation's combat (including the Final Assault / Hidden Core) can also be paused at any time with "Pause" in the top bar (or `Space`), stopping the countdown and the leader fight's overtime LP drain together (disconnecting while paused auto-continues, and the server finishes that combat for you). Combat in Alliance Simulation cannot be paused. "Abandon simulation": in Solo Simulation this ends the match immediately; in Alliance Simulation it counts as you being eliminated (operators return to the shared pool) and teammates continue. Restarting the server ends all matches.

**Rounds and draft rounds**

| Difficulty | Alliance: rounds / draft rounds | Solo: rounds / draft rounds |
|---|---|---|
| Standard Simulation | 14 / 3, 9 | 9 (round 9 is the leader) / none |
| Hazard Simulation | 14 (+15) / 3, 6, 9 | 14 (+15) / 6, 9 |
| Peril Simulation · Ultimate Simulation | 14 (+15) / 3, 9, 11 | 14 (+15) / 3, 9, 11 |

Higher difficulty means higher enemy attack / health multipliers, more dangerous enemies, and a higher leader health pool.

| Leftover funds | **Cleared at the end of the rest phase** (except with the strategy "Cantabile") |
| Recruit prices (by tier I–VI) | 2 / 3 / 3 / 3 / 4 / 4 |
| Refresh | 1 fund |
| Sell an operator | Any operator (including Elites) returns only **1** fund |

Gear cannot be sold, and gear in the Reserve can only be "destroyed" (no refund). | Other income | Bounty enemies, the alliances "Foresight" / "Miracle", the gear "Knight's Piggy Bank", and some strategies and traits |

The Dispatch Center level decides the **highest tier** the shop can show and the number of slots (Hazard and above: operators 3 / 4 / 4 / 5 / 5 / 5 slots + 1 gear slot).

## 3. Recruiting, the Reserve, and promotion

- **Buying takes two clicks**: the first click on a shop card only selects it (the card lifts and opens its details, and "Confirm purchase · click again" appears at the bottom), and only clicking **anywhere** on this card again (including the gold confirm bar) buys it; click elsewhere or press Esc to cancel. Upgrading the Dispatch Center and the promotion reward likewise take two clicks; the free picks granted by a strategy or gear (such as the 3 gear refreshed after Catherine's [Targeted Delivery] upgrade, Narantuya's [Share for All] 2 gear, or the Pager Module's 3 operators) also appear in the shop-card position, with their source written as the title on the left, gear shown as a gear card (icon, name, description, FREE), and you pick 1 for free; when there are several free picks at once they appear in sequence, and the hint button below the title and after "Later" says how many remain (such as "+1"). When you can't buy (for example when already Ready), clicking a card only opens its details.
- **Shared pool** (alliance): each operator has a limited number of copies, 12 / 14 / 18 / 16 / 8 / 5 for tiers I–VI, shared by all players; operators you hold (board + Reserve) occupy copies, while those shown in the shop do not. Once everyone has taken all of an operator's copies, it stops appearing; an eliminated player's operators return to the pool.
- **Position limits**: at most **8** operators deployed on the board; **10** Reserve slots; when the Reserve is full, newly gained units (merged Elites, rewards, returned gear, etc.) enter the **Temporary Reserve** (the 5 slots in front of the Reserve). While the Temporary Reserve holds units you **cannot ready up**, and the board outlines that row with a red dashed frame and explains the rule: put them into the Reserve or onto the field, issue or use them, or sell / destroy them. Units that enter the Temporary Reserve during a rest phase are auto-handled if still there at **the end of this rest phase** (operators returned to the pool, gear destroyed); those that enter only during combat, settlement or after readying up (such as battle rewards or Elites merged at settlement) are kept until the end of the **next rest phase**, so you always get a chance to handle them.
- **Elite promotion**: 3 operators of the same name (whether deployed or not) automatically merge into an **Elite** (greatly raising level, skills and module; Kazemaru needs only 2), and an Elite cannot merge again. If any of the merged operators is **already deployed on the field**, the Elite appears directly on **that operator's tile** (keeping its original facing, taking no extra deployment slot; when two or three are on the field, the earliest-deployed one is used — top-to-bottom, left-to-right); otherwise the Elite enters the Reserve (and the Temporary Reserve if full). The originally issued gear is all returned to the Reserve and must be re-issued; the merged operators' summons are removed with them, and an Elite on the field sends its own summons to the Reserve. When you select a mergeable shop card, the tile where the Elite will appear lights up with a gold frame, and the detail card that opens also states where it will go ("Promotable: the Elite will appear at its original spot in the combat area / will enter the Reserve"; the detail card auto-docks on the side that doesn't cover that tile). For merges completed at round start or settlement via strategies, rewards and so on, the Elite also plays the gold promotion effect as it appears.
- **Promotion reward**: after each promotion you get one special recruit: pick 1 for **free** from 3 random operators that are **one tier higher** (max VI) and **all different** (topped up from one tier lower when there are fewer than 3 left at that tier). It cannot be refreshed or frozen and expires at the end of the round.
- Each round the shop auto-refreshes the slots that aren't frozen.
- **"First manual refresh this round" is counted per operator** (Lappland): her effect takes effect on the first manual refresh you make this round while she is present, and it counts in the Reserve too — refresh first, then buy Lappland, and the next refresh still stacks her layer; an Elite merged this round from a Lappland that has already taken effect does not take effect again this round.

## 4. Formation and facing

- Drag an operator from the Reserve onto the board: melee / ranged tiles highlight as placeable. **You cannot deploy in water**: the water pool (deep-water zone) in the middle of Battlefield #08 (rising-tide control) refuses deployment just like officially, and no operator or summon can be placed there; raid-alliance operator redeploys and a Tactician's reinforcements auto-choosing a tactical point also only land on the bank. After you release, a **facing wheel** appears — swipe up / right / down / left, the board previews the rotated attack range with orange stripes, and release to confirm; release at the center or click "✕ click to cancel" to cancel.
- To only change facing: drag the operator back onto its own tile and pick a facing. Facing is kept until the next round.
- **Selecting goes by the tile on the ground**: clicking a tile selects the unit standing on that tile, and clicking an empty tile selects no unit (clicking the head drawn by an operator one tile back counts as clicking that tile behind). In combat, allies likewise go by tile; enemies walk between tiles, so clicking their body or near their feet (within 0.6 tile) works, and for a flying enemy click its body.
- While dragging, the model stays under the pointer / finger (feet about half a tile below the pointer, same for mouse and touch), and **the tile under the pointer** is the drop target and is highlighted; whichever tile gear is dragged onto, it is issued to the operator on that tile; releasing over the shop bar puts it back.
- Clicking an operator shows its attack range and the bottom buttons ("Retreat" back to the Reserve, "Sell +1"); right-click / long-press for details: next to the portrait is the alliance, and the first entry below is the operator's **trait** (such as "At the end of the rest phase …"), followed by characteristics, stats, attack range, skills, module, gear and talents. Stats are **live values**: refreshed 4× per second in combat (current health, attack, defense, arts resistance, attack interval, block count), and the rest phase shows a board operator's values **at the start of battle** (already including gear, alliance layers, traits, strategy and draft effects); above the base value is green, below is red with the difference shown (a shorter attack interval counts as a buff), and hovering shows the base value.
- **Summons are placed by hand**: after you put an operator with a deployable summon on the board, its summon enters the Reserve (Silence's medical drone, Magallan's curse doll, Catherine's 2 support devices, Skadi the Corrupting Heart's Seaborn, Vigil's wolf pack, Muelsyse's manifold; one operator's summons stack on a single slot and take no deployment slot), and you drag it onto the board and pick a facing like an operator. Catherine's support device gives a barrier to the operator on **the tile it faces**, so place it next to an operator and facing it. A **Tactician's reinforcements** (Vigil's wolf pack, Muelsyse's manifold — i.e. choosing a tactical point), as officially, **can only be placed within the summoner's attack range**: only those tiles light up while dragging; and when you later re-face the Tactician in place, reinforcements no longer in the new attack range return to the Reserve (those still in range stay put; when both the Reserve and Temporary Reserve are full and there is nowhere for the reinforcement to return, the re-facing has no effect and shows "Reserve is full"). Retreating or selling an operator removes its summons along with it; **moving an operator to another tile (including dragging a summon onto an operator to swap places) returns its already-placed summons to the Reserve**, and they must be re-placed. Summons returned while the Reserve is full enter the Temporary Reserve, and those not handled by the end of the rest phase return to the Reserve again next round.
- In combat: the Seaborn, wolf pack, manifold and support devices deploy at their placed position at the start of battle; the medical drone and curse doll also deploy once for free at their placed position at the start of battle (lasting 10 / 15 s, and like officially they don't need the skill to fire first), and afterwards appear again at that spot each time the owning operator casts its skill; if the owning operator is knocked down and redeployed, the one granted by the skill appears immediately if it hasn't appeared yet. When Magallan leaves the field the curse doll retreats with her, and when Silence leaves the field a medical drone that has already appeared is unaffected. The medical drone, curse doll, support devices and Seaborn do not appear without being placed; the wolf pack and manifold (a Tactician's reinforcements) appear at the tactical point when not placed.
- **Blocking** goes by contact: an enemy within about 0.7 tile of the operator's blocking center and in contact with it (ground operators can't block flying or low-hovering enemies). When an enemy is stuck between two operators, after the operator blocking it falls, retreats or is stunned, another operator still in contact with it and with a free block count **immediately takes over blocking**; only when there is no free slot does the enemy walk off. An enemy walking head-on stops in front of the operator's tile. **If you block it, you can always hit it**: any operator (including a ranged operator standing on a melee tile, and summons) can always attack an enemy it blocks, regardless of whether it is in the attack range or on which side of the operator, and it is targeted with priority (except Medics: Medics other than the Hexer still heal allies as usual while blocking an enemy and won't attack it; also during "stop attacking" type skills; and anything the skill text limits otherwise follows the text: Provence's S2 while active does not target enemies above 80% health, and Santalla's S2 drops ice shards at random on tiles within the attack range).
- At the start of battle operators deploy in **top-to-bottom, left-to-right** order, with summons after all operators. A blocked enemy attacks the operator blocking it first; an unblocked ranged enemy attacks the one with the highest taunt level first, and at the same level the one deployed last (summons, operators lower and further right, and just-redeployed operators are more likely to be hit). A concealed operator can only be attacked by enemies it blocks; **camouflage** (such as after Vulpisfoglia's S3 downs an enemy, or Kroos the Keen Glint's S1) likewise can only be attacked by enemies it blocks and is shown semi-transparent, but it does not count as concealment (it doesn't trigger "when concealed" effects like Siracusa's). Siracusa's concealment ends by time (32 s + 0.4 s per layer); Vulpisfoglia's camouflage lasts, per officially, until her next skill cast. **While Tippi has taken off** it blocks only flying enemies, and ground enemies won't target it (normal attacks, splash, skill ranges, burning zones and debuffs that need to select a target all miss it); flying and hovering enemies, and effects stating "ignores unselectable" (such as Contamination Corrosion), can hit it as usual; ground enemies' auras and field-wide effects (such as "Tombstone" halving all attack and defense) and the counters its own attacks draw (such as a Heavy Scout's Expose) still apply as usual, and damage-over-time already applied before takeoff still ticks.
- **Skills fire automatically** per the official "skill strategy": an ordinary manual skill fires when the skill is ready, an attack / heal is imminent, and an enemy is in the initial attack range (heal skills: an injured ally present); a skill **with its own skill range** (such as Texas's "Sword Rain", SilverAsh the Reignfrost's "Guardian's Edge", Degenbrecher's "Return to Serenity", not the "attack-range expansion" type) fires **as soon as an enemy appears within the skill range**, without attacking first; a **Defender's** manual skill fires on taking damage (exceptions: Underflow's and Liskarm's S2, Horn's S2 and S3, and Ashlock's S1 and S2 are changed by player feedback to fire as soon as an enemy is in the attack range — this is a deliberate difference from the official game; Underflow's S1 still needs to take damage); a Flagbearer / Tactician / Bard skill fires as soon as it is ready, and a Liberator / Phalanx Caster fires when an enemy is in the initial attack range. **Auto-triggered** skills follow their own rule — for example Gummy's "Emergency Rations" fires when an injured ally is nearby (within the skill range), turning her next attack into a heal of that ally (no need to engage first; her S2 "Cooking the Rations" is a manual skill and fires on taking damage per the Defender rule); Saria's "First Aid" fires only when her attack is imminent and an ally at no more than half health is nearby, turning that attack straight into a heal; Exusiai the New Covenant's S2 shield goes, per officially, to the ally with the **highest aggro** in the attack range (higher taunt level first, and at the same level the one deployed last), not necessarily the one taking hits up front. A manual skill's auto-cast has a 3-second cooldown: wait 3 s after deploying at the start of battle, and afterwards the same operator auto-casts at least 3 s apart (a chargeable skill won't fire all its charges at once).
- **Knockback / pull** is decided by the official "force − weight" for the distance: Wild Mane's S2 (medium force) for example pushes enemies of weight 0 / 1 / 2 / 3 about 2.1 / 1.7 / 0.4 / 0.1 tile, and weight 4 and up can't be pushed — lighter enemies are pushed farther. Wild Mane's S2 pushes along her facing: enemies within 45° directly ahead by the distances above, and enemies behind, to the side or adjacent get only a light nudge (within about 0.4 tile); Swire the Elegant Wit's S3 coins push outward from around her, landing on ground enemies and enemies she blocks within the 2–4 tiles in front of her (official "range 2-4"). Attack speed bottoms out at 20 (it won't go lower even when swamp, frost and similar slows are maxed).
- **The elemental damage bar** is right below the unit's own health bar: the element icon on the left, and the white bar is the remaining elemental value, which only empties on a real burst; after a burst the bar turns the element's color, the icon flashes, and it slowly refills during the cooldown (no elemental damage accumulates during this time).
- **Enemies**: a ranged enemy's range is measured, per officially, from the operator's collider (radius 0.25 tile), so it is 0.25 tile farther than the attack-range radius in the data — for example a Sarkaz Wither Tank (range 2.2) can hit an operator within 2.45 tiles of its center standing on low ground. Its first attack is "Corrosion Barrage" (leaving a radius-1.7, 10 s contamination zone at the target: 50 true damage per second on low ground, 25 on high ground, not stacking by the same name), and its normal attack damage doubles while blocked. 'Wings of the Sankta' and similar fly around erratically in a small area for 5 s the first time their health drops below half (the official "Fear"); the Fear caused by operators like Siracusa and Nymph makes enemies flee away from the operator. A concealed enemy can only be targeted while blocked (or revealed), and splash and a skill's circular AoE can't hit an unblocked concealed enemy either (same as officially): a Dublinn Flamechaser Soldier revives for 1 s each time it's downed, then becomes a concealed "Ember" that keeps walking forward (needs 5 hits to down, the Guard's "Fire Ash" 10), and you generally need an operator to block it to target it (it stops being semi-transparent while blocked), and it stands back up at full health if not killed within 10 s; slows, Fear and other states on an enemy are cleared when it revives and skill cooldowns restart, and a Feared Flamechaser can be blocked once it becomes an Ember; a Decode Basis α in its primal form takes zero damage from everything, and on its 4th physical hit, 4th arts hit, or when blocked, it spends 2 s becoming an Avenger, Special Tactician or Phantom, after which it can be downed. |
- **The strategy "Duck Lord" ("Mystery Customer")**: from round 5 on, each of your and your teammates' battles (**the Final Assault and Hidden Core count too**, and the Unite Phase does no separate swap) has 0–2 ground enemies (not leaders or their parts, nor bounty enemies) replaced by special versions of Duck Lord, Gopnik, Crying Thief or Fatty, appearing at the time and on the route of the replaced enemy (in a two-player leader fight, each side only replaces the enemies heading toward their own protection point); whoever downs one gets 1 fund (teammates in the Unite Phase count too), and leaking into the protection point still costs 1 target LP as usual. Duck Lord and Fatty **don't attack and cannot be blocked**: Duck Lord's move speed is +300% after taking damage; Fatty always faces the side with more of our operators and takes −80% physical / arts damage from the front. Crying Thief is concealed (targetable only while blocked) and its attack stuns the target for 3.5 s; Gopnik is immune to stun, freeze and levitate.
- The 🔍▶▶ at the top right lets you view the enemies about to attack this round (the preview area), and the 🔍 at the top left shows the match info (disabled alliances, disabled operators, strategies).

## 5. The Unite Phase (alliance)

Triggered in a round when **someone leaks enemies while someone else had a perfect combat** (total annihilation with no leaks): up to 2 teammates with a perfect combat bring their own formation (keeping the health and skill state from when their combat ended; operators already knocked down when combat ends lie on their tile, and per the official "forced exit" restart their full redeploy countdown and redeploy as usual once time is up and Deployment Points suffice — a short Unite Phase may not leave time to redeploy; a start-of-battle redeploy-time reduction such as the draft's "Conscription" counts too) to intercept all the enemies other players leaked.

- Participants are chosen by the official rule: most units on the field > has an active alliance > most un-downed units > earlier seat (independent of LP); when two participate, the one ranked first (next comparing the total layers of active alliances) "engages first" on the right half.

- Only teammates with a perfect combat can take part in the Unite Phase; **alliances do not stack layers** during the Unite Phase.
- Downing a bounty enemy in the Unite Phase pays funds to whoever downs it.
- Enemies still alive after the Unite Phase deduct LP by their original owner; with nobody having a perfect combat, it is deducted directly.
- A player who leaked sees an orange "×N" label in the middle of the top bar (next to the kill count): the number of enemies you leaked that are still on the Unite battlefield (including ones not yet spawned, **with no cap**), decreasing by one for each one a teammate downs (when an enemy that splits or summons is downed, the enemies it spawns count as yours too and the number rises accordingly); the target LP simultaneously shows the value after deducting min(N, 10). The row for a leaking teammate in the team panel on the left also shows ×N (updated in sync with the kills on the battlefield; hover to see whose leaked enemies they are).
- Each player loses **at most 10 LP** per normal round.

## 6. Alliances

An operator belongs to 1–2 alliances. A alliance activates once its **distinct-name** members on the field reach the required count (most count only board operators; "Foresight", "Miracle" and "Investor" count the Reserve too).

- **Core alliances** (8 factions: Yan, Sargon, Victoria, Kjerag, Laterano, Ægir, Siracusa, Kazimierz): 3 members to activate, 6 for a powerful extra effect.
- **Add-on alliances** (Precision, Swift, Dexterity, Arcane, Steadfast, Boost, Foresight, Miracle, Investor, Raid, Unyielding, Harmony, Garrison Operator, Lone, Mastery): most activate at 2 / 3 members; "Lone" needs **exactly 1** on the field, "Mastery" is counted by the number of Elites on the field, and an operator with no faction alliance counts as a "Garrison Operator".
- **Harmony**: when active, each core alliance that already has members on the field gets +1 to its member count (only +1, no matter how many Harmony operators there are). The alliance details show "incl. Harmony +1", and above the member list it lists the Harmony operator providing this 1 member (such as Muelsyse).
- **Pyrite's Blessing**: when there are 6 or more "Yan" operators on the field it summons Pyrite's Blessing (two at 9). It tracks the highest-aggro enemy on the field and hovers above it, and its skill "Flame of Banishment" locks onto 1 target and **spews fire continuously** (up to 20 s, dealing arts damage per second to enemies around the target with burn attached, not normal-attacking while spewing, ending when the target dies or is silenced); when there is temporarily no enemy on the field it waits in place. It has the official "Isolated": Medics won't heal it, and allies' buffs, auras and talents won't select it (enemies can still attack it).
- **Layers**: alliances stack layers via various effects, and more layers means a stronger effect (for example "Yan" attack rises with layers, and "Foresight" gives funds every 10 layers). **Layers are kept for the whole match but only take effect while the alliance is active**, and in most cases only an active alliance can stack layers. Each alliance has at most **999 layers** (same as officially, taken from a constant in the official client): past 999 any further layers gained are voided outright, and layer-based rewards (such as "Foresight" funds) stop accordingly. Layers gained in combat are shown **live** in the alliance panel, and after combat they are kept until settlement (they don't get refunded during the Unite Phase either).
- **How bonuses stack**: the "attack / defense / health +X%" from alliances, strategies, gear, draft cards and traits and a skill's "attack +X%" are **added together** and then multiplied onto the stat (official: direct multiplication) — for example Precision +130%, Lone +60% and a +40% piece of gear total +230%, not ×2.3×1.6×1.4. Damage multipliers such as "damage raised to X%" and "damage taken +X%" (Victoria, Kjerag, Arcane, fragility and the like) still multiply among themselves; but a same-name effect counts only once — when two players on the same battlefield both run "Arcane", the enemy carries only one layer of Arcane vulnerability, the higher of the two (no longer multiplied); two "Gnosis" permafrosts, and two "Reinhardt" / "Silent Texas" arts-resistance reductions, likewise count only the strongest one. "Steadfast" reflect damage is credited to the member taking the hit (visible in the per-person leader-damage stats).
- Each match randomly **disables** a number of alliances (fewer in Standard Simulation), and their operators won't appear in the shop; the opening "Confirm match info" lists them, and you can review them again during the strategy draft ("View disabled alliances and operators") and in-match (🔍 at the top left).
- Two same-name pieces of gear merge into a stronger piece, and specific gear combinations can grant an operator an extra alliance effect: when "Polymorphic Isomer" is carried together with another alliance piece (such as a Vice Hammer), the bearer counts as a member of that gear's corresponding alliance. The correspondences are in the "Talents" column of the Polymorphic Isomer's detail card (alliances disabled this match are marked; while equipped on an operator, the active set is highlighted).

## 7. The Final Assault and the Hidden Core

- **The Final Assault** (round 14; round 9 for Solo Standard): players are paired two by two by seat, each pair shares a battlefield with one defending the left half and one the right (the right player's board is mirrored left-right). Everyone **chips away at the same leader's health bar** (health = that difficulty's official leader health, taken from the current version's game data, e.g. Ultimate "OpFor: Armor" 3,600,000, regardless of how many survive), and you can't see the other pair's battlefield. The whole team's LP is merged into one pool, with no more 10-per-round cap. After the 120-second countdown at the top ends, combat **continues** (no timeout loss); from the 150th second of battle (real seconds) the whole team loses 1 LP per second. Reaching zero leader health is a win (the leader is already down when the bar shows 0 and combat ends immediately), and reaching zero LP is a loss — whichever hits zero first decides it, and damage dealt after LP is exhausted no longer counts. Alliances do not stack layers in this phase.
- **Damage cap** (the Final Assault and Hidden Core, same as officially): when a **single hit** on the leader reaches 300000 or more, the whole hit is voided — that hit deals 0 damage, is not counted on the leader's health bar, and shows no damage number; it is not clamped to 300000 but counts for nothing at all (299999 works as usual). It only targets the leader itself (including the other leader in a two-player battlefield), while the leader's parts, guards, drones and other small enemies are unaffected, and normal rounds and the Unite Phase are unaffected too; when a part transfers damage to the leader by the same amount, or a drone's death makes the leader lose 2% health, that too is checked as a single hit (currently at most 144,000, never tripping the line). With extremely high layers the leader can still go down in a few seconds (for example when both of each player's alliances are at 999 layers, or every active alliance is at 999), which comes from the official layer-based, uncapped damage multipliers (Kjerag, Arcane, Precision, etc.), and the official community has a "boss dies in one second" saying.
- The rest phase of the Final Assault / Hidden Core decides placeable tiles by **your half of the end-stage battlefield** (the right player mirrored left-right), and the tiles highlighted while dragging are the placeable ones: for example in the "Originium Current Generator" end-stage map the tiles surrounded by the 1×3 orange pipe can be deployed on. Operators placed on these tiles in the Final Assault stay in place when entering the Hidden Core.
- **The leader's parts**: when a drone of "OpFor: Armor" dies (no matter who downs it; leaks don't count), Armor loses 2% of the leader's total health bar (e.g. Ultimate 3,600,000 → 72,000 each; the official game doesn't say which "max health" this refers to, and this project treats it as the total health bar, which is inferred); the Hidden Core's "Armor Cleaver / Armor Crusher" and "Gunshatter" transfer every point of damage they take onto the leader's health bar by the same amount (the blade / hammer always count once downed; hits taken mid-dive count too, a point where the official text is inconsistent in two places, which is inferred). The blade / hammer normally hover at Armor's side, invincible; once thrown they blink to a fixed route and charge the lowest-attack operator, and being hit 15 times en route makes them 【Paralyzed】 (stunned 10 s, grounded, taking ×1.3 damage); after landing or the paralysis ends, they turn their remaining health into a new one back at the original spot. "Cataclysm" fires only 1 Armor Piercer each time (even below 20% health), hitting only the highest-attack operator in the attack range.
- **Giant leaders** (OpFor: Armor / Pipe, Sal Viento Bishop Quintus, Alistair - Final Flame of the Empire, 'Samivilinn,' the Resolution of the Very North) have a hit area of about a 5×3-tile region (the whole pipe block): an attack range touching any one of those tiles can hit it; they are **self-bound and cannot be blocked**, and won't leave their spot. Splash is judged by the enemy's center, so splash landing on a small enemy beside it can't reach the giant leader.
- **The Hidden Core** (round 15, Hazard and above only): after the leader is beaten, if the team's total active alliance layers are **> 1200** (Solo > 350) and LP > 1, you enter the Hidden Core to challenge a stronger leader. A loss doesn't affect clearing the match.

## 8. Emotes and spectating

- "Chat" at the bottom left opens the emote panel: 6 themes × 6 emotes, swipe left / right (or arrow keys) to change the theme. An emote shows as a bubble next to your portrait for 3 seconds, with a 1-second send cooldown.
- During the rest phase, or after your own combat ends (showing "Combat over, waiting for teammates to finish"), click a teammate's portrait on the left → "Go watch" to watch their battlefield; you can't spectate while your own combat is in progress. In the Unite Phase and the Final Assault you can use ‹ › to switch left half / panorama / right half.
- After being eliminated you can freely watch any battlefield.
- **Whoever you watch, the alliance panel shows their alliances**: while viewing a teammate's base or battlefield (the rest phase, after combat ends, or auto-spectating after elimination), the alliance panel at the top left switches to **that teammate's alliances, counts and layers**, with an amber "👁 teammate's name" tag in front, and the alliance details you open also say "teammate's name's alliances" (the member column only marks their operators on the field, not in the Reserve); in combat the layers grow live (matching what they see). In the Unite Phase and the Final Assault it follows ‹ ›: when the camera is on a teammate's half it shows their alliances, and on your own half it shows your own; in "panorama", if you are fighting on this battlefield it shows your own, and if you aren't on this battlefield (watching the Unite Phase after leaking, or spectating after elimination) it shows the teammate you selected with "Go watch", or the first player of that battlefield if you never selected one (the 1st Uniter / this pair's first player), and **never shows your own**. Clicking a teammate's portrait on such a battlefield → "Go watch" moves the camera straight to their half, and the "👁 teammate's name" on ‹ › matches the alliance panel's tag; next to ‹ › there is "Back to battlefield" (absent when eliminated). When the alliance panel switches person, a alliance detail opened from the alliance panel closes automatically. When you open the detail card of an operator on the field, the alliances on it (and the alliance details opened from it) belong to **that operator's owner**, regardless of whom the alliance panel currently shows — for example clicking a teammate's operator in "panorama" shows "teammate's name's alliances" with their counts and layers, while clicking your own operator on a teammate's half shows your own. Back at your own base / battlefield it returns to your own alliances. When watching a teammate after combat, refreshing / reconnecting still returns you to the teammate's battlefield being watched, with "👁 teammate's name" and "Back to battlefield" still there. An eliminated teammate no longer shows alliances (and cannot be viewed).

## 9. Settlement titles

At settlement, titles are awarded by performance (at most one per player, each title to only one player): **Star of the Garrison** (highest leader damage, only on a win), **Immortal Alliance** (highest total active layers), **Rock Solid** (least LP lost), **Elite Gathering** (most Elites promoted), **Fully Equipped** (most gear issued), **Spendthrift** (most funds spent).

## 10. Tips

- Early on, prioritize assembling one alliance you can activate over buying scattered high-tier operators; 3 of the same name auto-promote and bring a free high-tier recruit.
- Funds can't be kept to the next round: refresh or upgrade whatever you don't use; when you want a card but can't afford it, **freeze** first.
- Upgrade prices drop −1 each round automatically, so if it's not urgent you can wait a round or two.
- During the rest phase, check the enemy preview at the top right; if there are flying units keep anti-air (ranged) operators ready; **low-hovering** enemies (Skimming Sea Drifters, Lucky Flyfin) count as air units too, and melee and ground-only operators (such as Rosmontis) can't hit them until you stun, freeze or put them to sleep (a Skimming Sea Drifter crawls after it drops, and the model also changes to a crawling pose).
- In Alliance Simulation don't fight teammates over operators of the same faction (the pool is shared); dividing the work to assemble different alliances is easier.
- When LP is down to around 10 (at most 10 lost per round), prioritize keeping a perfect combat.
- The Unite Phase only fields teammates with a perfect combat, so a formation that reliably clears enemies is valuable to the whole team.
- Entering the Hidden Core looks at the team's **total active layers**: late game you can stack layers deliberately.
- At 999 layers a alliance is full and extra stacking does nothing, so you can move resources to another alliance.
- In leader fights watch the **damage cap**: a single hit of 300,000 or more is voided entirely. After a alliance is stacked to 999 layers, piling on extra damage multipliers (for example lighting Arcane with 999 layers of Kjerag) can instead make your big skill deal 0 damage; a teammate's Arcane and other debuffs also amplify your damage.
- **Tiles fenced in orange** (official "walls") can hold operators but enemies can't walk in: an operator standing inside attacks enemies in its own attack range as usual (pick a facing when placing so the attack range faces the enemies' path), but **can't block ground enemies** (an enemy pushed or pulled to the fence's edge isn't blocked by it either), and is rarely hit — most Defenders' manual skills need to take damage to fire and may never fire inside (Underflow's S2 is an exception, firing when an enemy is in the attack range); an operator that only hits the tile right under it (such as Cardigan) can't hit any enemy inside.
